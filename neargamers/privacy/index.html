Privacy Policy

Effective date and last updated: 6 November 2026

This policy explains how NearGamers for Android (com.neargamers.app) handles information in version 0.3.0 (version code 7). NearGamers is published by Denny John, an individual developer using the DJ Apps Studio name. In this policy, “I” refers to Denny John.

1. How NearGamers works

NearGamers offers board games with nearby players on compatible Android phones and solo games against an on-device opponent. Core gameplay can work without internet access. A player profile is created locally. New game access uses a separate Google-account licensing identity: activate an independent 30-day demo, then optionally choose a paid Google Play plan. Cached valid access permits offline play.

Information is handled in four ways: game records stay on your phone; nearby multiplayer sends information to other players' phones; explicit account licensing uses the NearGamers service; and Google Play services may collect the Nearby SDK diagnostics described below. If you email support, I also receive the information you choose to send.

Account licensing and optional subscriptions

The separate demo is one server-timed 30-day period per Google account for this app, without payment details, a subscription or automatic paid conversion. Reinstalling and account recovery retain the original dates. Optional monthly/annual subscriptions charge the displayed Google Play price and renew until cancelled through Google Play. Paid cached access includes 30 days after the verified paid-through date; the demo does not receive another 30 days.

Explicit activation, recovery, purchase verification or restore sends a Google identity token, Play Integrity evidence, installation public key and, when relevant, a purchase token over HTTPS to the dedicated NearGamers licensing service. Identity-token claims are processed to authenticate the account; Google profile names and pictures are not retained by the licensing service or automatically used as your nearby avatar. Pseudonymous account identifiers, original demo dates, purchase-ownership hashes and bounded admission counters are stored in its separate Firestore database. After disclosed verification, the enabled support directory retains verified sign-in email, NG- Support ID, original dates, last verification and a dated verified purchase summary. No game profile, match, move, chat, rating, nearby identity or backup is uploaded. Tokens and private signing keys are not shown in the dashboard.

Ordinary launches and game actions use an encrypted signed installation-bound cache outside backups. Normal reboots retain access; suspicious clock rollback may require online verification. Failed checks retain the existing signed deadline. An offline certificate cannot receive immediate remote revocation. The authenticated owner dashboard is read-only, with isolated per-app identifiers and no purchase/entitlement mutations.

Use Demo & account to verify and request licensing-account deletion, even without active paid access. Identifying support-directory details are removed; limited pseudonymous original-demo and purchase-ownership eligibility records remain to prevent reset or reassignment. Uninstalling removes local data but does not reset the account demo or remove server eligibility records. Cancel subscriptions separately in Google Play. If verification is unavailable, contact support.djapps@gmail.com with the displayed Support ID; never send passwords, identity tokens or complete purchase tokens. See the account deletion instructions to request deletion without reinstalling the app.

2. Information kept on your phone

  • Your profile: your chosen display name, built-in avatar or selected gallery-photo thumbnail, and a stable random player identifier. You can use a nickname; a legal name is not required. Renaming your profile does not change the identifier.
  • Device identity and opponents: an installation identity key pair and fingerprint, opponent profiles and trusted-device records used to recognize previously verified devices. These opponent records are created by the app, not taken from your phone's contacts. The private identity key stays on your phone.
  • Multiplayer records: match participants, name and avatar snapshots, selected games, moves, results, timestamps and confirmation or recovery information, used to play, resume matches and display history.
  • Ratings and standings: locally calculated results and rankings. Existing opponent ratings are not exchanged or merged between phones.
  • Practice records: solo game history, with up to 100 records, saved solo positions, same-phone game positions and series, offline challenge progress, and a saved solo Air Hockey match state, including scores and difficulty.
  • Preferences and safety: tutorial progress, appearance and sound choices, favorites, recent games, community-rules acceptance and local block records.

Core gameplay does not request your email address, phone number, physical address, date of birth or contacts. A gallery avatar is optional. Android's photo picker or document picker gives access to the image you select; the app does not request broad access to your photo library. It crops and re-encodes a small thumbnail without copying the original metadata, then stores that thumbnail locally. The original photo is not uploaded to the licensing service. The thumbnail is shared with connected nearby players as part of your profile and may remain in their opponent and match records. You can still disclose personal information by typing it into a display name or optional chat message; nearby recipients receive that text.

3. Discovery before connection approval

When nearby discovery is active, your display name and shortened installation identity fingerprint are advertised to nearby discovering devices before a connection is approved. Connection approval therefore does not keep these discovery details private. Choose a display name you are comfortable making visible nearby.

Discovery may restart while the app is open to recover a saved session. Advertising and discovery stop when the app goes into the background. New devices use a connection-approval and verification flow; previously trusted devices can be recognized for reconnection.

4. Information exchanged with nearby players

Connected players exchange display names, avatar selections (including a selected photo thumbnail), stable player identifiers, installation public identity keys and fingerprints, and the game information needed for their shared session. This includes participant and match identifiers, moves, results, timestamps and confirmation or recovery information. Identifiers help recognize opponents and reconcile matches; a random identifier should not be treated as anonymous.

Participating phones may retain opponent profiles, trusted-device records and shared match history locally. In group Ludo, which supports up to four players, the host distributes the roster and relays relevant session messages to the other participants. Information can therefore reach all players in the match, including through the host's phone.

NearGamers does not upload your profile or game history to a publisher-operated server. Sharing with nearby players still transfers information off your phone, even when the game works offline.

5. Optional match chat

You may send text messages of up to 500 characters to current match participants. Message text and author and message identifiers are sent to their phones. For group Ludo, the host receives and relays group messages. Chat has no photo, file, audio or video attachments; optional avatar thumbnails are shared separately as described above.

The app keeps a bounded list of recent messages temporarily in memory, rather than saving chat in permanent game history. Changing the match bound to chat or losing the app process clears that in-memory list. A temporary disconnect does not necessarily clear it. Other players may independently copy or screenshot messages, and their copies are outside the app's temporary retention controls.

Chat is optional. Community rules must be accepted before nearby sharing. You can hide a message locally, block a player on your phone, and prepare an email report. Blocking stops communication with the recognized player on your phone; it does not remove content from other phones or impose a global ban. Block records persist locally until you unblock the player.

Reporting opens a draft in your email app or lets you copy the draft. You review the recipient and contents, choose whether to include the selected message, and send it yourself. The draft can include the selected reason, your details, player identifiers and match context. Opening or copying a draft does not submit it. NearGamers does not automatically upload reports or maintain an in-app report archive. Email providers process sent reports, and the support correspondence practices in section 10 apply.

Support cannot investigate reports, remotely remove content, intervene in matches or impose global bans. There is no staffed moderation service or promised report-response time. Use blocking for immediate local control and avoid sending private or sensitive information to other players.

Optional voice input opens an Android speech-recognition activity. The app receives recognized text for you to review before sending; it does not record or store audio itself. It asks the recognizer to prefer offline recognition, which does not guarantee offline processing. The recognition provider controls microphone access and any online processing under its own permissions and privacy practices. Typing remains available without voice input.

6. Nearby permissions and location

Nearby multiplayer uses Google Play services Nearby Connections, which can communicate using Bluetooth, Bluetooth Low Energy and Wi-Fi. The transport used depends on the devices and Android version.

  • Bluetooth permissions support scanning, advertising and connecting to nearby devices.
  • Wi-Fi state, control and nearby Wi-Fi access support discovery and local communication.
  • Legacy location permissions support discovery on older Android versions: coarse location on Android 8–9 (API 26–28), and fine location on Android 10–12 (API 29–31). The app checks whether the system location setting is enabled where discovery requires it.
  • Network-state access is used by the Nearby dependency.

Modern Bluetooth scanning and nearby Wi-Fi permission declarations state that they are not used to derive location. NearGamers does not obtain, store or transmit latitude or longitude for gameplay. Legacy permission requirements do not mean that the app uses GPS coordinates. Android and Google services have their own data practices.

You can review or revoke permissions in Android settings. Denying required permissions prevents or limits nearby multiplayer; solo play is available without another player.

7. Google SDK diagnostics

The reviewed version uses Google Play services Nearby Connections 19.3.0. Google's Nearby Connections documentation says the SDK collects usage analytics to improve the service: discovery and connection latency, reliability and throughput, together with device model, country, build version and application package name. This provider collection is separate from your local game records and the information sent to other players. Country information is not described here as GPS coordinates.

Google says you can allow or deny this collection in Android Settings → Google → Usage & diagnostics. Google controls its diagnostics handling and retention; this policy does not promise a deletion period for Google-held data. See the Google Privacy Policy for its practices and controls.

NearGamers uses a dedicated publisher-operated licensing backend and Firestore database, separately described above. Game records have no cloud save, advertising, first-party analytics or crash-reporting service. The app requests internet access for explicit account licensing and purchase verification; Google Play services can also carry out its own documented diagnostics activity. Locally packaged open-source licence notices use Google's OSS Licenses component; Google's core Play services disclosure states that this component does not collect end-user data. That statement does not remove Nearby's separate diagnostics collection.

8. Retention and deleting local data

Profiles, trusted-device records, multiplayer history, ratings and preferences normally persist through app restarts and compatible updates. There is no automatic time-based expiry for those records. Solo history retains at most 100 records, replacing older records as the ledger grows. Air Hockey keeps the current saved match. Chat follows the temporary in-memory limits in section 5.

To remove the app's local data, use Android Settings → Apps → NearGamers → Storage → Clear storage / Clear app data, or uninstall NearGamers. Setting names vary by device. The app has no in-app control to delete individual histories, trusted opponents or all local game data. Licensing-account deletion is a separate control in Demo & account. Reinstalling and creating a new profile can create a new player identifier.

Android cloud backup and app-data device transfer are disabled. You can deliberately export a practice backup containing saved practice positions, same-phone series and challenge progress, or a read-only history archive. You choose the destination through Android, and the receiving storage provider handles the exported file under its own practices. Practice import previews replacement of the included local practice data before you confirm it. These files do not transfer your private identity key, trusted devices, blocks or ranked ratings; a new installation must be verified with nearby players again. Deleting app data does not delete exported files, which you must remove separately from their chosen destinations.

Deleting data on your phone does not delete copies on other players' phones, copied text, screenshots, support correspondence or Google's diagnostics. I cannot remotely access your local app records or erase copies held by other players. Use Google's controls for Google-held information and the contact below for correspondence you sent to me.

9. Security

NearGamers uses Android app storage and permission controls. Its private installation identity key stays on the device, and peer verification helps authenticate connections. Google documents encryption for established Nearby device-to-device connections. This does not establish that pre-connection advertisements are private or that every Google diagnostics transfer has been independently assessed.

No independent security certification or complete all-traffic encryption assessment is claimed. No device, application or wireless connection can guarantee absolute security, and recipients can keep information they receive.

10. Support and privacy enquiries

Anyone may send a support or privacy enquiry to support.djapps@gmail.com. Only Denny John can access this support inbox. Sending an email provides your email address, message and any attachments you choose to include. These are used to review and respond to your enquiry. Email providers also process correspondence under their own policies.

There is no fixed retention period for support emails. Correspondence may remain in the inbox after an enquiry is answered; there is no automatic 90-day deletion commitment. You can email the same address to ask about, correct or request deletion of correspondence I hold. I will review your request and explain any limits, including applicable legal obligations. This does not delete copies held by your email provider or other recipients.

Please send only the information needed for your enquiry. Contacting support does not give me access to your phone's local profiles, game history or other players' copies.
