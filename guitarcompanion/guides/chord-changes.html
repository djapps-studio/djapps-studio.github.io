How to practise guitar chord changes

By DJ Apps Studio · Updated 10 October 2026 · 5 min read

A smooth chord change starts with a clear shape and an unhurried movement. Practise two chords at a time, then give the change a steady beat. Here is a five-minute routine you can repeat with a guitar and a timer.

1. Make each chord sound clear first.

Start with two shapes you already know, such as Em and Am. Fret one chord and pick its intended strings individually. Listen for a note that buzzes or a string that does not ring. Place your fingertips close behind the fret wire and check that they are not brushing a neighbouring string.

Use only enough pressure for the notes to sound clearly. Keep your shoulder and wrist relaxed. If a position hurts, stop and adjust it. A faster change will not fix a shape that is uncomfortable or unclear.

2. Rehearse the movement without a beat.

Move slowly from Em to Am, then back. Look at where each finger needs to land. Lift only as far as you need to clear the strings and try to place the next shape together. If your chosen fingerings share a finger on the same string and fret, that finger can stay in place; different fingerings may use a different route.

After each change, strum once to check the result. Repeat five times in each direction. Aim for a familiar movement before trying to make it quick.

3. Try this five-minute practice routine.

A starting routine for Em and Am
TimeExerciseListen for
Minute 1Pick the intended strings in each chord.Clear notes, with no unintended muted strings.
Minute 2Change slowly, without a metronome.A relaxed movement and a clean landing.
Minutes 3–4Set 4/4 at 60 BPM. Strum once on beat 1 and change chord for the next bar.The next shape ready for the next beat 1.
Minute 5Strum on all four beats, still changing once per bar.A steady pulse through the change.

Sixty BPM is a starting suggestion. If you keep missing beat 1, slow down or spend another minute on silent changes. Keep counting while your fretting hand moves. Once several bars feel comfortable, add 5 BPM and check again. There is no required speed to reach in one session.

What to do when the change keeps breaking.

  • The first strum buzzes: stop the timed exercise and check the landing string by string.
  • You pause before every change: lower the tempo or allow more beats on each chord.
  • One direction is harder: spend a short extra block on that direction, then return to alternating chords.
  • Your hand feels tense: release your grip, rest briefly and restart with less pressure.
