WristScribe Privacy Policy

Version 10 | Updated 16 October 2026

Version 10 adds explicit Google sign-in, server-side licensing, installation-bound certificates and licensing-account deletion. This version describes the Google-account licensing revision; earlier installed releases may use earlier access flows.

Who provides WristScribe

WristScribe is provided by the publisher identified on its Google Play listing. For privacy questions and licensing-data deletion requests, contact support.djapps@gmail.com. This policy describes the Android phone and Wear OS watch apps. It also explains where an Android service or another app handles information under its own privacy terms.

Information on your devices

WristScribe keeps journal text, titles, entry identifiers, timestamps, favorites, deletion markers and synchronization state in private app databases. The apps also store appearance and recording-language/output preferences and phone journal-selection preferences and watch recording-queue/synchronization records. The phone stores the version of the User Agreement you accepted. Phone demo activation and paid licensing verification use explicit Google sign-in. Existing-content viewing/export/deletion and free watch capture do not require a licensing account. WristScribe does not include advertising, tracking or analytics SDKs. WristScribe does not upload journals, transcripts or microphone audio to a developer-operated server. Agreement acceptance is stored locally and is not sent to the publisher.

Google sign-in and licensing

Only when you explicitly activate/recover a demo, verify/restore a purchase, or verify account deletion, the phone obtains a request-bound Google identity token. The licensing server validates its signature, audience, issuer, expiry and nonce. Identity tokens may contain Google profile information; the server uses the stable account subject and does not persist names, email addresses, profile pictures or raw identity tokens. No Firebase Authentication/App Check session, background authentication refresh or scheduled licensing polling is used.

The phone sends its installation public key, proof of its private-key possession and, for access issuance, a request-bound Play Integrity token. Google processes app/device integrity information under its policies. The server checks package, Play app-signing certificates, recognition, Play licensing and device integrity. The installation private key and clock-integrity key stay in Android Keystore. The certificate and integrity-protected clock anchor are stored privately, excluded from backup/transfer and journal backups. Ordinary launches and feature use verify them locally. An untrusted reboot/clock state requires explicit online verification. Expired demo results remain local and do not cause launch-time polling.

The dedicated Google Cloud licensing service stores app-scoped keyed Google-subject hashes, immutable trial dates, purchase-ownership hashes and bounded anti-abuse counters. It never receives journal text, transcripts or microphone audio. Google Cloud processes ordinary connection metadata such as IP addresses to serve and protect the endpoint. In-memory IP burst keys are keyed hashes with bounded memory/window; application code does not persist raw IP addresses. Platform operational logs can contain request metadata. Ordinary request logs are configured for seven-day retention; mandatory provider audit retention can differ.

Google Play billing

The phone uses Play Billing to display localized base-plan prices and initiate purchases. Google handles payment-card and payment-account information. For explicit verification, the purchase token is sent over HTTPS to the licensing backend and used in memory with Google's subscription API to check product, account binding, state and expiry and acknowledge a valid purchase. Raw purchase tokens are never intentionally logged or stored by the app or licensing database. The database retains keyed purchase-token ownership hashes, including linked-purchase ownership, to prevent reassignment. Paid certificates last no more than seven days and never beyond verified entitlement expiry. Offline refund/revocation delay is bounded by the remaining lease. Existing unbound purchases require verified support migration.

Reviewer access

A Google Play reviewer may enter the existing publisher-provided code on phone Premium to enable a separate local review grant. The code is checked locally and is not saved, logged or uploaded. Only its verifier identifies the local grant, separately from paid/demo evidence. Remove it on the same screen. Reviewer preferences are excluded from backup/transfer and never sent to Wear. Reviewer access itself requires no Google sign-in or licensing request.

Licensing-account deletion and retention

Use phone Premium > Delete licensing account at any time, including after expiry, and sign in with the same Google account to prove ownership using a fresh nonce. This also works after reinstall without journal restoration. The public instructions are https://djapps-studio.github.io/wristscribe/delete-account.html. Contact support.djapps@gmail.com if the normal flow is unavailable; never send passwords, identity tokens, purchase tokens or journal contents by email. Ownership verification is required before account action; a claimed email address alone is not sufficient.

Verified deletion removes ordinary account/installation records. The current minimal schema does not store profiles or installation lists. App-scoped keyed account hashes, original demo start/expiry and purchase-ownership hashes remain for the licensing service's lifetime solely to enforce the original trial and prevent purchase reassignment. These hashes are pseudonymous personal data, not anonymous data. Admission counters/nonce hashes are targeted for cleanup after seven days; provider TTL cleanup is asynchronous and chargeable. Support correspondence remains in the support mailbox only as needed to resolve the request and meet applicable obligations. Account deletion does not cancel Google Play subscriptions, delete local journals/exports or immediately revoke certificates on other offline installations. Manage those independently. Different Google accounts have separate demos. No unbreakable device/person identity or tamper resistance is promised.

Microphone and transcription

Speech capture on the phone or watch uses the microphone when you start or resume a recording session. You can also create phone entries with the keyboard without microphone access. WristScribe passes speech to the device's Android recognition service and displays the returned text. WristScribe does not write microphone audio to files, keep audio recordings or offer audio playback. For the device-default language it prefers on-device recognition where supported; an explicitly chosen language and the fallback path use the configured system speech service with an offline preference. That request is not a guarantee: the installed service may send audio and related information to its own servers. Processing, retention and network use by that provider follow its settings and privacy terms. English-letter output converts recognized script locally on the recording device to approximate Latin spelling, without a translation service or additional network transfer. Unsupported characters are retained; typed text and existing entries are unchanged. Transcripts are saved when you finish; canceled sessions are not saved to a journal. To help recover an interrupted capture, each app keeps one bounded text draft in private storage excluded from backup. Draft writes are briefly coalesced, so the latest words may still be lost during abrupt termination or storage failure. Recovered text opens paused; recovery does not start the microphone. Finishing or explicitly discarding clears the draft. Drafts older than seven days are rejected and removed when recovery is next checked; this is not a timed secure-erasure guarantee. No audio is stored.

Between your watch and phone

Paired devices exchange text recordings, timestamps, random identifiers, checksums, durable receipts and selected appearance/speech settings through the Wear OS Data Layer. Phone Premium gates receipt of new recordings. No purchase tokens or entitlement flags are sent to the watch. The phone chooses a journal at first receipt; new phone captures remain local. Older whole-journal transfers and phone handoffs are supported for upgrade recovery. Platform services handle Data Layer delivery; WristScribe adds no developer-operated journal server.

Optional reminders and shortcuts

You may enable local journal or backup reminders and choose an hour between 08:00 and 21:00. These are off initially, use Android notification permission, and contain generic prompts rather than journal text. Android may delay or suppress reminders. WristScribe stores your reminder choices, last notification times, completed-capture count and last successful manual-backup time locally. Reminders never create a backup automatically. An optional phone home-screen widget opens voice or typed capture and shows no journal content.

Export, share and manual backup

Choosing Copy entry places the selected text on Android's clipboard. WristScribe marks it as sensitive to suppress content previews on supporting Android versions. Clipboard retention and access by the operating system or another app follow that platform's behavior; copying is not an encrypted export.

Sharing prepares temporary text attachments in the phone's private cache and grants the chosen receiving app access to the selected files. Attachments older than 24 hours are removed when another share is prepared; Android can clear cache earlier. The receiving app controls any copies it keeps.

Exports create text, Markdown or ZIP files at the destination you choose. Manual WristScribe backups include journals and selected preferences, but not device-lock credentials, agreement acceptance or recording-language/output preferences. Backups are checksummed for integrity and are plaintext, not encrypted. Android's file picker may offer local storage or installed providers such as Google Drive. WristScribe does not sign you into Drive or request access to your entire drive. The chosen provider controls its copy and any network transfer. No automatic backup is provided; the apps disable Android automatic app backup and exclude journal databases and files from device transfer.

Restoration reads and validates the file you select, previews it, and merges or replaces phone journals. Before replace, WristScribe writes an emergency backup in private phone storage. It keeps the latest emergency copy until a later replacement overwrites it or app storage is removed. This is not an off-device recovery service. Remove shared, exported and backup files through their destination apps or providers when you no longer want them.

Retention and deletion

The phone retains the journal library in private app storage. The watch keeps at most 20 new unsynced recordings and shows the latest pending recording. When full, it stops capture without overwriting old recordings. Subscription expiry pauses new receipt and does not delete queued recordings. Older unsynced upgrade content is preserved even above the limit. The watch clears recording text only after an exact phone persistence receipt, then removes its retained Data Item with retryable cleanup. Small IDs, hashes, timestamps and receipts remain for idempotency; this is not a secure-erasure guarantee.

Phone recording receipts survive local journal edits, deletion and backup replacement, so a retry does not recreate received content. These internal receipts are not included in manual journal backups. Deleting a journal hides it locally; text and markers can remain in the private database. A newer legacy whole-journal revision may still make an older removed journal visible again. No sync operation deletes exported or backup files. Uninstalling or clearing app storage normally removes private journals, preferences, receipts, cache and emergency backups on that device. Copies retained by recipients or document providers must be managed there separately.

Protection and choices

Android's app sandbox and device protections apply to private storage. WristScribe does not add its own encryption to its databases or backup files, and a checksum is not encryption. You can optionally require your phone's device credential to open journal content and block screenshots and recent-app previews. WristScribe does not receive or store your PIN, pattern, password or biometric data. These options do not protect exported files or prevent privileged access to the device.

You control microphone permission in Android settings. Denying permission prevents speech capture; other supported text and journal features remain available. You choose when to share, export or back up information and which destination receives it.

App update checks

Both apps can automatically retrieve a small public release catalogue from WristScribe's GitHub Pages website over HTTPS, no more than once a day after a successful check while a non-recording screen is open. Failed automatic checks wait at least an hour before another attempt. The request has no journal, transcript, microphone audio, purchase token, reviewer code, device identifier or version query parameters. GitHub receives ordinary connection information, including the network IP address and a generic WristScribe update-check user agent, under the GitHub Privacy Statement linked below. WristScribe adds no analytics or tracking to this endpoint.

The apps store the automatic-check choice, last attempt/success times and bounded public release metadata locally, separately from journal backups. Automatic checks are enabled by default and can be turned off in phone Settings > About & Legal > App updates or watch Connection > Guide and legal > App updates. You can still check manually. Checks never block recording or install an app. An available update opens Google Play only when you choose it; account, region and track eligibility can affect availability. Google Play controls download and installation and its own auto-update setting independently.

Public website

The WristScribe information and policy pages are hosted on GitHub Pages. We add no analytics, advertising, cookies, contact forms or journal uploads to these pages. GitHub receives connection information needed to serve the website and handles it under the GitHub Privacy Statement. Following a Google Play link opens Google's service under its own terms. The website cannot access your app journals.

Policy updates

Version 10 introduces the Google-account licensing and deletion behavior above and requires renewed phone User Agreement acceptance once. Acceptance remains local and does not activate a trial or subscription. Earlier app releases may present a prior agreement version. Journal/audio privacy and free watch capture are preserved.

For support or privacy questions, use the publisher contact under App support on WristScribe’s Google Play listing.
